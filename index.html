How this works (the part worth learning)

Stage 1 — Independent answers. The same question goes to three models in parallel. Diversity matters: models trained by different labs make different mistakes.

Stage 2 — Peer critique. Each model receives the two rival answers (anonymized, so no brand bias) and is asked to find errors and omissions. This is the "check each other's work" step — in the literature it's called multi-agent debate.

Stage 3 — Judgment. A fourth model sees everything — question, answers, critiques — and scores each answer. This pattern is called LLM-as-judge.

Known limitation: models share training data, so they share blind spots. Cross-checking catches sloppy errors well, and shared misconceptions poorly. Watch for cases where all three agree and are all wrong — those are the most instructive.
